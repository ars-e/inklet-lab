What's The Point?

Scroll

This is where I sketch the outline of things. Who I am, what I’m doing now, what this website is, and the idea of Inklet Lab. It’s the context and the compass, the thread tying the other pages together, a working note that shifts as I do. That’s the point of The Point: to keep the story alive while it’s still being written.

The Point of Origin

I can’t tell my story without first telling you another story.

We need to talk about The Point!, an animated film based on Harry Nilsson’s concept album. The story is simple: in a land where everyone must have a literal point on their head, and where everything must have a “point,” a round-headed boy named Oblio is banished for being “pointless.” Sent into the Pointless Forest with his dog, Arrow, he discovers that everything has a point even there...just not always the one that fits a template.

If you would like to explore more about the film, check out this thoughtful piece: What’s ‘The Point!’?: The Forgotten Genius of Harry Nilsson

Why this fable, and why here? Because it mirrors the way I have moved through life, not without purpose but without forcing a single fixed definition too early. Like Oblio, I have found meaning in my winding path: in following questions, in carrying ideas across different worlds, in acquiring new skills, and in shaping my point through time and practice. Without this simple parable as a key, the story of who I am, and what Inklet Lab is and is becoming, cannot be fully understood.

The Point of Me

(tl;dr)

I’ve been a computer science graduate, a civil services aspirant, and most recently an economics post-graduate.

I’m a curious student at heart, always ready to pick up new tools, ideas, and ways of thinking. I stretch, adapt, and do whatever it takes to work through the problems in front of me.

Born and raised in Hyderabad, shaped significantly by my years in Delhi.

The Point of this Place

So, what is this place? It’s two things: my personal corner of the web, and the seedling of a larger idea called Inklet Lab.

My Corner of the Web

At its core, this is my personal website. It gives a glimpse of my work, thinking, and experiments in connecting the dots across different fields and a standing invitation for conversation.

The Seedling of Inklet Lab

Inklet Lab is the most exciting, and least defined, part of this journey. The vision is to build an ecosystem where ideas meet, a place to find your lab partner. Think of it as a “Tinder for ideas”, a space where people can both plant and pick up sparks.

Ideas here don’t have to fit into one box. They can come from anywhere: art, science, design, philosophy, policy, education, technology, or even just a stray thought from daily life.

If you have an idea, you can post it and and look for a lab partner, someone who wants to pick it up, test it, and shape it with you. If you’re browsing, you can explore ideas left by others and become their lab partner, jumping in where your curiosity or skills connect.

But Inklet Lab is meant to be more than just a matchmaking board. The larger vision is to create an ecosystem, a kind of lab beyond the metaphor; where collaborations don’t just spark but are supported. A space with the tools, conversations, and shared energy needed to take ideas further than any one person could alone.

Unlike an incubator, there are no rigid tracks, no pressure to “scale.” This is not about turning every idea into a startup. It's about giving ideas of all kinds a place to breathe, play, and evolve with the right partners.

Right now, Inklet Lab is just a seedling, an experiment I’m nurturing through this site. But the vision is clear: a living lab where ideas find partners, and partners find the space to build.

(The why)

The need for this space came from two directions.

First, after finishing my master’s and stepping into the job market, I wanted a place to showcase myself beyond a neat CV or LinkedIn line. Something that felt less like a résumé and more like an extension of who I am; a glimpse of my capabilities, curiosities, and the ways I approach problems.

Second, the Inklet Lab idead itself. The idea of building an ecosystem for cross-pollination—needed a ground to grow. A space where rough drafts, false starts, and half-formed sparks could live alongside finished work.

(The How)

In that spirit, this website is an artifact of its own philosophy: a small, handcrafted corner of the internet. It was built not with complex frameworks or heavy machinery, but with the fundamental tools of the web. The structure is plain HTML, the styling is done with Tailwind CSS and traditional CSS, and the interactive is powered by vanilla JavaScript.

This site owes a lot to the spirit of the Indie Web. If you’re curious, read about it here.

Much of this website is vibe coded, but never carelessly. The design was carefully shaped, with each choice guided by instinct as much as intention. For readers curious about how I see vibe coding, I find myself aligning closely with the reflections shared here.

Since this site is an extension of myself, most elements (even the smallest ones) carry some form of inspiration or meta-reference. A not so obvious example would be the advertising posters on "The dispatch” page nod to accounts Don Draper handled in Mad Men and a very obvious example would be the dramatic title on "The Balcony” page mirrors the iconic “Superstar” opening in Rajinikanth films. Almost every corner has its own quiet nod to a story, a world, or an aesthetic that shaped me.

The Longer Version of the Biography

My story was supposed to follow a familiar script: excel in school, pursue engineering, and settle into a linear, predictable career. For a while, I followed it without a second thought.

Going off Script

However, my first taste of life away from home, at a college that decided to experiment by teaching social sciences alongside engineering, began to offer an alternative script.

I was surrounded by people with ambitions, a concept so new to me I hadn't realized it was something a person could have, let alone be nurtured.. I had brilliant professors whose ways of thinking and teaching, subconsciously and profoundly, impacted how I learned and studied. Suddenly, I also had access to the wider world of ideas through the internet which, though only sometimes high-speed, was a significant upgrade from what I had back home. My world cracked open, granting me access to ideas, knowledge, and tools I never had before.

While I had a natural grasp of code and a mind that bent toward mathematics, I felt a growing disconnect. I can't pinpoint a specific moment, but something shifted in me during those four years of college. The 'why' behind the 'what' became more compelling. I wanted to understand the human side of systems: why societies function as they do, how choices are made, and what connects individual decisions to collective outcomes.

Here, I made my first major detour from the script. I chose to prepare for the Indian Civil Services, seeing it, at that time, as the most direct way to work on the complex, human-centered problems I was becoming so aware of.

Formative years

Until this point, like most, the only major learning I had was that exceptional grades and a top job placement were the only paths to a desirable life. I was deemed ready for the world basis my age and intellect.

I had no practice in dealing with the consequences of my decisions in relation to major life choices and very little time or space to really get to know myself - no real clarity on what it is that will give me true meaning and fulfilment; something that is generally learned through trial and error, but I had been shielded from it.

So when I decided to prepare for the Indian Civil Services, it was, if I’m honest, an act of both naivety and arrogance. I had never really “failed” before, and perhaps because of that, I carried no conscious fear of failure. I didn’t know what the preparation entailed, had never met anyone who had gone through it, didn’t even know where to begin. Yet I leapt in anyway, moving to Delhi with little more than conviction. It was like plunging into the sea to catch fish withoutknowing the waters, the nets, or even what I was chasing.

To capture the experience fully would take pages. To keep it short: the first year was mind-numbing, spent learning the terrain, understanding the vast syllabus, and figuring out how to navigate a world that seemed to sprawl endlessly. The years that followed were intense. The pandemic arrived in the middle of it all, magnifying the uncertainty, the isolation, and the sheer emotional weight of the journey. But those years, demanding and often overwhelming, shaped me in ways no linear career path could have.

Like college, the new city brought new people, ideas, and knowledge, expanding my world in a completely different direction. It was a plunge into a different kind of depth, one that nurtured a restless, almost “polymath-ish” curiosity.

This was when I had to live with the consequences of my choices, confront crises of different magnitudes, and most importantly,fail. I failed many times, often alone. But in those failures, I learned resilience, self-knowledge, and the slow, painful craft of rebuilding piece by piece.

The Economic(s) Choice

Along the way, economics took hold. It felt natural, a language that spoke to both sides of my mind by providing a rigorous, mathematical framework to make sense of the messy realities of human behavior. It was the bridge I hadn't known I was looking for.

I decided to pursue a Master’s in Economics, carrying with me the discipline of an engineer and the breadth of a social science generalist.

Those two years not only equipped me with new tools to study the world and the self, but also gave me the space to return to coding in a serious way. I began to see how the technical and the human could inform each other, rather than exist apart.

Looking back, this did more than expand my intellectual toolkit; it gave me the confidence to step into the job market with perspective, and a profile that felt not just employable, but whole.

Stories & Chaos

Growing up without a TV at home, my daily escape was reading anything and everything I could get my hands on. That habit gave me a lifelong love of narrative. To make up for the missing screen, my parents took me to the movies almost every weekend, and those trips only deepened my fascination with stories. Books, films, comics, art; all of them showed me that meaning could be built from fragments, that narrative was a way to bring order to chaos.

For a long time, I thought I lacked creativity because I couldn’t draw or compose music. But slowly I realized creativity wasn’t about magical talent, it was about what stories had already taught me: collecting raw material and finding patterns in it. The more diverse the inputs, the more connections I could make. My creativity lives in asking simple, story-like questions: what if this idea belonged in that plot? what happens if I place this character in that setting? In other words, mixing ideas until a new narrative emerges.

Curiosity and idea-stacking, though, aren’t enough on their own. The hardest part is beginning; pulling a single thread from the chaos and following it until it takes shape. That act of starting is what stories, creativity, and chaos have in common: they all demand trust that the puzzle will eventually make sense. And in that process, I’ve learned that analysis and creativity aren’t opposites. They’re both ways of shaping disorder into meaning, both powered by curiosity and play. Even the most serious work, I’vefound, has space for a little imagination.

Now & Ahead

Now, I find myself at another turning point. I no longer see service as tied to a single channel, like the civil services I once pursued, but as something that can take many forms. Building, solving, and contributing can happen across research, startups, or organisations that value problem-solving and the ability to connect dots.

What matters to me now is that the work is concrete, challenging, and meaningful. The venue for creating value may change, but the core mission remains the same: to apply thoughtful solutions to human needs.

That, I’ve come to realize, is the point of me so far. Not a straight line but a roundabout path, one that keeps accumulating skills, perspectives, and-stories that shape the person writing this now, and the one still becoming.
